It always arrives at the same time. Everyone's in bed, the kitchen is finally quiet, and someone remembers the swimming money. Or the non-uniform day. Or that Thursday has a parents' evening in it that nobody wrote down.

The problem was never that we were disorganised. It was that the week lived in several heads at once, and only one of them was awake at ten o'clock.

## What the twenty minutes actually is

Sunday, after dinner, before anyone starts a film. One person reads the week out loud, Monday to Sunday. That's it.

The reading out loud is the bit that works, because it's the only version where everyone hears the same week. A shared calendar you each glance at on your own is useful. A shared calendar someone reads to the room is a plan.

:::checklist{label="The whole routine"}
- Read the week out loud, Monday to Sunday
- Name who is doing each thing, out loud, not in your head
- Put the money, forms and kit in the bag on Sunday, not on the day
- Agree the one night nobody schedules anything
:::

## Say who, not just what

"Swimming, Wednesday" is a fact. "Swimming, Wednesday, you're on pickup" is a plan. Most of our Sunday arguments were never about the diary. They were about the gap between knowing something was happening and knowing it was yours.

So we name a person for everything that needs one. If nobody can take it, you find out on Sunday, when there's time to ask a grandparent or swap a shift, instead of at 8.40 on Wednesday.

:::quote
The reset didn't make us busier or freer. It just moved the panic to a time when we were both awake enough to deal with it.
:::

## Pack it on Sunday

Anything that has to physically leave the house this week gets dealt with now: the envelope with the trip money, the signed form, the recorder that's lived under the sofa since June. Bags by the door on Sunday night means Monday morning is just shoes and coats.

It sounds small. It's the part that saves the most shouting.

## Protect one empty evening

The last step is the one we skipped for months, and the one I'd now fight for. Pick one weeknight and agree that nothing goes in it. No clubs, no admin, no "quick" favour. When the week fills up, as it always does, that evening is where the slack lives.

## What we stopped bothering with

A few things didn't survive:

Colour-coding everything. It looked lovely for a fortnight. Now each person has one colour and that's the end of it.

Planning meals in the same sitting. It turned twenty minutes into an hour, and nobody would sit through an hour. Meals got their own ten minutes on a different day.

Doing it perfectly. Some Sundays it's four minutes over the washing-up. That still counts.
