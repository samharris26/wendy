It's the moment on a Tuesday when you realise the swimming kit needs washing before Thursday, the dentist hasn't been booked, and there are four days until a birthday party with no present. Nobody asked you to hold all of that. You just do.

That's the mental load: the job of noticing, remembering and planning everything a household needs. It's real work, it's usually done by one person, and it almost never shows up on anyone's list of chores.

:::stat{figure="60%" source="ONS, Women shoulder the responsibility of unpaid work, 2016"}
more unpaid work is done by women than men in the UK, across cooking, childcare, housework and transport
:::

The Office for National Statistics put the gap at [60% more unpaid work](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/articles/womenshouldertheresponsibilityofunpaidwork/2016-11-10) on average, and the remembering is a big part of it.

## Why "just ask me" doesn't help

The well-meant answer is "just tell me what to do and I'll do it". It sounds fair. The trouble is that working out what needs doing, and then asking, is the job. The person asking is still the manager. The person doing is still the helper.

## Write the job down

You can't share something that only exists in one person's head. So the first step is boring: get it out. Every recurring thing the house needs, written somewhere everyone can see. Bin day. The car's MOT. School dinner money. The cat's flea treatment. Grandma's birthday.

Most couples who do this are surprised by the length of the list, and by who wrote most of it.

## Hand over whole jobs, not tasks

"Can you pick up a present for Saturday?" hands over a task. The remembering, the checking and the reminding all stay with you.

"Birthday presents are yours now" hands over a job. That covers noticing the invitation, putting the party in the calendar, buying the present, wrapping it and finding the card. It's a bigger ask, and it's the only one that actually lightens the load.

Good jobs to hand over whole:

- Birthdays and presents for one side of the family
- Everything to do with one child's club or activity
- The car: MOT, service, insurance, tax
- A whole category of shopping, such as toiletries or school uniform

:::quote
Doing a task is the easy half. Noticing it needs doing, and remembering, is the half nobody sees.
:::

## Then stop checking

This is the hard part for whoever used to carry it all. If you hand over birthdays and then quietly check whether the present's been bought, you've kept the hardest half of the job: the worrying.

Things will be done differently. Occasionally something will be late. That's the price of it being genuinely someone else's, and it's usually worth paying.

## Keep it where you can both see it

A shared list is what makes this stick. When jobs have a name next to them and a date, nobody has to be the one who asks. It's just on the list, and it's clear whose it is.

That's most of why I built [Noa](https://www.asknoa.app): tasks you can assign to a person, repeating reminders for the things that come round every year, and one place where the whole house can see what's coming up. A whiteboard in the kitchen works too. What matters is that it isn't only in your head.
