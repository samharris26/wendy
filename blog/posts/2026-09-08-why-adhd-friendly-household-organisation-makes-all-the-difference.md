## The results of planner overload

The diary was already full by the time we hit April. Notes, lists, and reminders splayed across its pages like a slow-motion avalanche. Six different-coloured pens did their best to add some order, but let's face it—a planner is no match for life in an ADHD household. You know the feeling. Like holding back the tide with a sieve.

Generic advice often tells you to "just use a planner," as if a single, organised book could tame the chaos. Here's breaking news: planners are not ADHD-wizardry. They assume your brain functions like a well-oiled machine, when life is chaos and hyperactivity wraps you like a blanket. The enemy is not organisation; the enemy is the belief that there's one silver-bullet solution. Spoiler: there isn’t!

## Externalising memory with the shared family calendar

When you've got ADHD, your mind resembles a circus—with all its distractions and competing attractions. Instead of trying to juggle it all inside your head, externalise those mental post-it notes. Enter the [shared family calendar.](https://www.asknoa.app/features/shared-family-calendar) This isn't just a digital notebook; it's your brain on screen. Birthdays, bin collections, or the vet's appointment. Everything goes in there. Most importantly, it isn't just yours. Everyone sees it, and they can all pitch in.

With everyone from seven-year-old Ruby to dear old Mum contributing, the calendar becomes a shared scene of collective responsibility. Imagine never having to be the sole juggler of three ring performances. That's liberating.

## Make capture friction disappear

The fewer hoops you jump through to log something, the better. Reducing the capture friction is crucial. You need it to be as instinctive as checking your phone. No more "oh, I’ll remember that," only to lose it in the daily fog. This is where the [WhatsApp assistant](https://www.asknoa.app/features/whatsapp-assistant) could be your new best mate. Instead of finding a pen, a scrap of paper, or unlocking apps, send tasks straight to your household system with a quick message. Forget zero-inbox. We're chasing zero-delay.

Consider it like having a PA who doesn’t ask questions, just takes notes.

## Set alarms, not intentions

Boring? Maybe. Effective? Absolutely. Alarms over intentions every single time. The intention to "pick up the dry cleaning at lunch" dissolves like the morning mist when the next shiny (or necessary) thing appears. Pop an alarm on for the time you realistically think you’ll have five spare minutes. Trust those alarms to keep you on track more than any promise you make to yourself.

## Self-compassion and progress over perfection

Finally, let's be real. You're not a robot; you're a wonderfully imperfect human with a complex brain. Self-compassion is more than a buzzword—it's a coping strategy. Perfectionism is the monster under the bed that eats your achievements.

ADHD facilities within homes are not about hyper-organisation. They're about achievable, functional spaces where you feel dignified, empowered, and...less frazzled. Take one step towards progress—it's leagues better than waiting around for a parade to celebrate perfection.
