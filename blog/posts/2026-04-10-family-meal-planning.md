It's five o'clock, everyone's hungry, and someone asks what's for tea. You open the fridge as if the answer might be written inside. It isn't. It's half a pepper and some yoghurt.

Meal planning has a reputation for being a Sunday-afternoon project with colour-coded spreadsheets. It doesn't need to be. The version that sticks takes about ten minutes a week.

:::quote
Meal planning isn't about cooking better. It's about not having to decide at five o'clock.
:::

## Write down your fifteen favourites

Most families rotate through the same dozen or so dinners, and that's fine. Write them down once: the meals everyone will actually eat, the ones you can cook on autopilot, and a couple of slightly more ambitious ones for weekends.

Now planning is choosing from a list, not inventing from nothing. It's much quicker, and nobody has to be inspired on a Sunday night.

## Plan five nights, not seven

Planning every single night sets you up to fail. Plans change, someone's out, there are leftovers. Plan five dinners, leave one night for leftovers, and keep one for whatever happens.

:::checklist{label="A week that survives real life"}
- Five planned dinners, the quickest ones on your busiest nights
- One leftovers night
- One emergency dinner that lives in the cupboard or freezer
- Batch-cook one of the five and freeze half
:::

## Match meals to the week

Look at the calendar before you plan. The night with swimming until seven is not the night for a slow-cooked stew. Put the quick meals on the busy nights and the longer ones where there's time. This is where meal planning and [the Sunday reset](/blog/sunday-reset-routine) fit together nicely.

## One shared shopping list

The plan falls apart when the shopping does. One shared list on everyone's phone fixes most of it: whoever uses the last of the milk adds milk, right then. Whoever's passing a shop can see exactly what's needed.

Write the week's ingredients onto the same list when you plan, and do one main shop. Mid-week top-ups become a quick look at the list instead of a guess.

## Keep two emergency dinners

Some nights, the plan will fail. Keep the ingredients for two meals that need no thinking in the cupboard or freezer at all times: pasta and a jar of sauce, fish fingers and peas, whatever your house will eat without complaint. Replace them when you use them.

If you like it on paper, the planner has the week's dinners on one side and the shopping list on the other, so you can plan and write the list in one go.
