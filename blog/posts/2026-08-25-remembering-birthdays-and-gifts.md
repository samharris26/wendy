Most people don't forget birthdays. They remember them on the day, in the kitchen, with no card, no present, and a sinking feeling about the second-class stamp.

The date isn't the problem. The problem is everything that has to happen before it: noticing it's coming, thinking of a present, ordering it, finding a card, posting it. Those steps start about two weeks early, and nothing reminds you.

## Step one: every date in one list

Start with a single list of every birthday and anniversary you want to mark: family, godchildren, close friends, the neighbour who feeds the cat. It takes twenty minutes, mostly spent texting relatives to ask when Auntie's birthday is.

Put them all in your calendar as yearly repeating events. Write them once and you'll never type them in again.

## Step two: remind yourself two weeks early

A reminder on the day is too late for anything that needs posting. Set each birthday to remind you two weeks before, which is enough time to think, order and post without rushing.

For the handful of people you always do something bigger for, add a second reminder a month out.

:::quote
You don't forget birthdays. You forget that a card needs posting four days before one.
:::

## Step three: keep a gift-ideas note, all year

The best present ideas turn up in March, when someone mentions they've broken their favourite mug. By November they've gone.

Keep one running note, a section per person, and add ideas the moment you hear them. When the two-week reminder comes up, you're choosing from a list rather than starting from nothing.

## Step four: buy cards in bulk

This one's unglamorous and works brilliantly. Buy a box of ten or twenty assorted cards and a book of stamps, and keep them in one drawer. Half of the birthday panic is the trip to the shop for a card.

## Share the list

If you share birthday duties with a partner, share the list too. Decide who's doing which side of the family, and put their name on those reminders. There's more on handing over whole jobs in [the invisible job](/blog/the-invisible-job-mental-load).
